Is there ice in the Moon's polar craters?

The problem

NASA's Mini-RF radar sees a high CPR (circular polarization ratio: echo that kept its "twist" ÷ echo that flipped) inside some polar craters. Ice can do that — but rough, rocky ground also gives high CPR. So a bright radar crater alone does not prove ice.

Step 1 — Check on Earth

Canada's RCM radar uses the same design as Mini-RF. We measured CPR where we know what is on the ground.

One clue is not enough, so we stack three independent maps of each crater from NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter.
