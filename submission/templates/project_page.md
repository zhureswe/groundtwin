## Project Details

**What it does.** GroundTwin treats Earth as the twin of the Moon and Mars, in two directions.

1. **Earth analogs of the Moon and Mars (main direction).** The Analog Engine scores each of Earth's $n_cells land cells ($cell_deg°) against a Moon south-pole profile and two Mars profiles (Gale and Jezero craters). It uses seven physical features from NASA data: dryness (GPM), absence of plants (MODIS NDVI), day–night temperature swing and cold nights (MODIS LST), dust (MODIS aerosols), terrain roughness and elevation (USGS GMTED2010). Users choose a mission mode (Science, Rover operations, Base construction, Ice & resources) and see what matches, what does not, and what Earth can never reproduce. We tested the method on $n_known known analog sites from NASA and USGS. Known Mars analogs rank on average in the top $mr_gale% of Earth for Gale (p = $mrp_gale; random ≈ 50%). In a blind leave-one-out test $bt_mars_k of $bt_mars_n were recognised (chance ≈ $bt_mars_chance). Our field case is Mangystau, Kazakhstan: the floor of the ancient Tethys Sea, with layered sediments, salt flats and stone concretions. It ranks in the top $mgs_gale% of Earth's land cells for Gale. That is above the Utah (MDRS, San Rafael Swell) and Devon Island references in our table, and we found no scientific study of Mangystau as a Mars analog.

2. **Checking radar ice signatures on Earth for lunar base siting.** NASA's Mini-RF radar on LRO sees high CPR (circular polarization ratio) in some polar craters, but rough rock also gives high CPR. Canada's RCM satellites use the same hybrid compact-polarimetric design, so we measured CPR over Earth sites with known ground truth. Median CPR is $smooth_median for smooth ground, $rough_rock_median for rock fields and $ice_median for glacier ice. For $n_craters lunar south-pole craters we combine Mini-RF contrast, LOLA floor slope and Diviner temperature into an Ice Evidence Score from 0 to 3. $n_ies2 craters reach at least 2; this means "consistent with ice", never proof. We also tested the published criterion CPR > 1 and DOP < 0.13 on Earth. In C-band it barely separates ice ($pol_ice% of cells) from lava ($pol_rough_rock%), so one radar clue is not enough.

**How it works.** A Python pipeline (`pipeline/01–14`) downloads open data from Google Earth Engine, PDS and the RCM archive. It computes every number and writes small JSON files. The static website (GitHub Pages, no server) reads only these files. It computes similarity scores live in the browser, so users can switch targets and modes. The site includes a polar crater map with the "Earth radar ruler", the analog map with top places, the Mangystau page, Method and Limitations pages, a CesiumJS 3D globe with links to Google Earth and NASA Moon/Mars Trek, and "Ask GroundTwin", a help box of prepared answers (not a live AI).

**Benefits.** It is free, open and needs no login. It helps landing-site and analog-testing teams pick Earth sites by measurable features and see where each analogy breaks. It gives lunar radar readers an Earth reference measured with the same radar architecture. Every number is traceable to a file, and the limitations are stated openly.

**Tools.** Python (numpy, rasterio, rioxarray, pyproj, shapely, scipy, matplotlib, earthengine-api), Google Earth Engine, HTML/CSS/JavaScript with Leaflet, Chart.js, KaTeX and CesiumJS, and GitHub Pages.

## Use of Artificial Intelligence
We used Claude (Anthropic) through Claude Code as a tool for writing code (Python and HTML/JavaScript), processing data and editing English texts. The idea, the choice of sites, all checks of the data and results, and the design are our own. No AI-generated images, videos or audio are used: all figures are plots of our data and the screenshots are of our own website. The short "By our numbers" place descriptions and the help-box answers were drafted with AI assistance from our computed data and are labelled on the site.
